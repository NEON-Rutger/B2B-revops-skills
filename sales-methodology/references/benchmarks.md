# Sales Methodology Benchmark Data

Last verified: 2026-09. Figures older than 2025 are kept only where no newer edition exists and are marked as such.

Benchmark data on discovery, multi-threading, qualification, and negotiation, drawn from the Ebsta x Pavilion 2025 GTM Benchmarks and the Fullcast/Pavilion 2026 benchmark set (Fullcast acquired Ebsta in August 2025; the 2026 dataset covers $78B pipeline, 361,000 opportunities and 2,500 reps). All figures are vendor platform data unless marked otherwise. Use these to pressure-test your process discipline and to quantify the cost of skipping stages.

Headline finding: sales efficiency declined 28% year on year. Deal value fell 11.1%, win rates dropped 13.5%, and sales cycles lengthened 6.9% (Fullcast/Pavilion 2026, as reported by SaaSletter: https://www.saasletter.com/p/ai-nrr-gross-margin-efficient-frontier-2026-gtm-sales-benchmarks-fullcast-pavilion). Those three compound. The teams that held the line did it through execution discipline, not more activity.

## Stage discipline: skip rates

The share of deals where reps bypass a critical stage. Higher is worse.

| Stage | Skip rate | Read |
|---|---|---|
| Qualification and discovery | 59% | Most deals are worked without being properly qualified. |
| Solution validation / alignment | 52% | Validation with the buyer is routinely skipped. |

Source: 2026 GTM Benchmark Report skip rates as quoted in secondary coverage; the primary report page could not be retrieved in the 2026-09 pass. The earlier per-stage figures for Proposal (40%), Discovery as a separate stage (38%) and Negotiation (25%) could not be traced to a source and were removed.

Discipline pays: forecast accuracy improves from 48% to 94% when built on execution discipline rather than activity volume (Fullcast 2026 Benchmarks Report, https://www.fullcast.com/ebooks/2026-benchmarks-report-the-state-of-gtm-in-2026/).

Qualification quality is measurable: only 36% of deals that pass Discovery carry both a qualification score and supporting notes (Ebsta 2025 Sales Qualification Report, 655,000 opportunities, $48B: https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/new-ebsta-report-reveals-top-sales-teams-win-6-3x-more-often-by-qualifying-smarter-not-harder-302520694.html).

## Multi-threading: win rate by stakeholder count

Win rate rises from about 0.2x with a single relationship to 2.6x with 10 or more engaged stakeholders (Fullcast 2026 GTM Benchmarks Report, as quoted by Fullcast: https://www.fullcast.com/content/account-based-growth/). Single-threaded deals are now a liability, not a convenience.

| Stakeholders engaged | Relative win rate |
|---|---|
| 1 | 0.2x (lowest) |
| 10+ | 2.6x (highest) |

Intermediate bands (2-3, 4-6, 7-9 stakeholders) were previously listed at 1.1x, 1.4x and 2.1x; they could not be verified against a published source in the 2026-09 pass and were removed. Expect a monotonic rise between the endpoints and measure your own bands.

Relationship depth matters as much as headcount: coverage without relationship depth is not multi-threading (practice-based; the earlier "relationship score 91-100 wins at 2.2x" figure could not be traced and was removed).

## Discovery and qualification: what good looks like

- **ICP alignment is the single biggest lever.** Going after the wrong customers cuts the chance of closing by up to 75% (Fullcast 2026 Revenue Benchmark Report: https://www.fullcast.com/content/fullcast-releases-2026-revenue-benchmark-report-analyzing-78-billion-in-revenue-data/). Disciplined qualification against a real ICP is worth more than any closing tactic.
- **Well-qualified deals are 6.3x more likely to close**, win at 50% versus 8% for poorly qualified deals, and close 21.6% faster (Ebsta 2025 Sales Qualification Report).
- **Balanced pipelines are 57% more likely to close** than overloaded ones (Fullcast 2026). A rep drowning in pipeline qualifies nothing and wins less.
- **Expertise-based routing** (matching buyers to reps with the right experience) raises success rates by up to 40% (Fullcast 2026).

These are the numbers behind the SPICED scoring gates. A deal that has skipped qualification is statistically a worse bet, regardless of how it feels on the call.

## Negotiation and cycle time

- **Lost deals consume 2.0x more selling days than won deals** (Fullcast 2026 Benchmarks Report). Secondary coverage puts this at roughly 225 selling days for a loss versus 115 for a win; treat the day counts as indicative and the 2.0x ratio as the verified figure. Slow-dying deals are the most expensive thing in the pipeline.
- **Momentum decays with time.** Deal momentum is the clearest predictor of outcome (Fullcast 2026). The earlier bucketed decay figures (1.9x in the first 30 days falling to 0.6x after 361 days) could not be traced to a source and were removed; build the same curve from your own closed deals.
- **Deal size and efficiency.** The earlier "deals above $70K run at 2.1x efficiency, 6.4x more efficient than sub-$15K" figures could not be traced to a source and were removed. Measure efficiency by ACV band in your own data before deciding whether to qualify up.

## How to use this in deal reviews

1. Check skip rates against your own funnel. If qualification or solution validation is being skipped, that is the constraint, not closing skill.
2. Count stakeholders per open deal. Anything single-threaded at late stage is a red flag the benchmark quantifies at 0.2x.
3. Watch deal age against your own won-deal cycle. A deal running at twice your median won-deal cycle is statistically closer to the lost-deal pattern (lost deals take 2.0x the selling days) than the winning one.
