# Enablement Frameworks

Eight frameworks that together form an enablement architecture; the Willing, Capable, Enabled adoption diagnosis; and the full coaching protocol. The slim SKILL.md keeps the summaries; this file holds the operational detail.

## The eight frameworks

### 1. Traffic Light Classification
Classify every change by impact before designing communication.
- **Green** (low impact): a written update is enough. Minor field change, new report, updated docs.
- **Yellow** (medium impact): written plus video. Process tweak, new dashboard, updated SLA.
- **Red** (high impact): minimum three touchpoints, written and video and live training. New methodology, comp change, territory redesign, tool migration.

Most organizations default to Green for everything. That is why adoption fails. Force the classification before any communication ships.

### 2. Reverse Salient (coaching focus)
Coach only the one skill whose improvement would move the most revenue. Selection method: step 2 of the coaching protocol below.

### 3. Coaching protocol
A six-step sequence that builds Capable. Detail: "The coaching protocol" below.

### 4. Organizational Energy as Infrastructure
Adoption does not coast on willpower. It needs sustained organizational energy: daily scores visible to reps, constant conversation about the one thing, public tracking of progress. Design the energy system, not just the content: visible scoreboards, daily standups referencing the one thing, a dedicated channel, manager 1:1s structured around it, public recognition. If the organization is not talking about it daily, the change is already dying.

### 5. Data-Led Diagnosis as Discipline
Deconstruct the entire funnel monthly, not because something is broken, but as a standing discipline. Proactive diagnosis has surfaced eight-figure win-rate opportunities in funnels whose win rates already sat at a healthy 38 percent. Build a monthly diagnosis rhythm into the operating cadence before anything breaks. The best coaching targets come from data, not intuition.

### 6. Systems Shift Narrative
Change management is not just implementing a new process. It is shifting which self-reinforcing loop the system runs on.
- **Decaying loop:** bad data, unreliable forecasts, gut-based coaching, inconsistent behavior, worse data (accelerating decay).
- **Compounding loop:** clean data, reliable forecasts, data-driven coaching, consistent behavior, cleaner data (accelerating growth).

Name which loop the team is in, and frame the change as the switch from one to the other.

### 7. Productivity Dip Communication
Productivity dips before it improves. Leaders must say, out loud and in advance:
1. "We expect a dip in the first 30 days."
2. "Here is why the dip happens" (learning curve, new habits).
3. "Here are the leading indicators that tell us we are on track despite the dip."
4. "Here is the timeline for when we expect to beat our previous baseline."

Without this narrative, executives reverse the change at exactly the moment it is about to work.

### 8. Path of Least Resistance Design
The best change management makes the new behavior the easy choice.
- Embed the new process in the tools (required CRM fields, automated prompts).
- Remove friction from the desired actions.
- Add friction to the old behaviors.
- Design the system so doing it right is easier than doing it wrong.

## Willing, Capable, Enabled

The adoption model this skill uses. A change sticks when people are Willing, Capable and Enabled at the same time. Miss one leg and the change does not stick.

| Leg | Definition | Test question | Who fixes it |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Willing** | The internal motivation to want the new state | Do they bring it up themselves? | Leadership and the early group: the why and the visible win |
| **Capable** | The skill to perform at the level the new state needs | Can they do it without asking you for the prompt, the script or the answer? | The frontline manager, through coaching |
| **Enabled** | The systems, tools, access, data, time and environment that make it possible | Is what they need one click away? | RevOps |

### Two failure patterns
1. **Pushing down on Willing.** Rollouts usually ignore Willing and press on it instead: "you have to do this." Compliance shows up for a few weeks. Then attention moves on and the old behavior returns.
2. **Forgetting Enabled.** Rollouts usually forget Enabled and find out late. The field is missing, the access request is still pending, nobody has time to practice. The rollout needed more enablement than anyone planned for.

### Diagnose before you coach
Coaching fixes Capable only. Coaching an unwilling rep, or a rep whose tools or time block the behavior, wastes the session. Run the three tests for the person or the group before booking any coaching.

| Leg broken | What you see | Quick check |
|---|---|---|
| Willing | The rep has the tools and the opportunities, yet does not attempt it live. Never raises it in 1:1s. Asks whether it is still a priority. | Ask what they think the change is for. A blank or cynical answer points to Willing. |
| Enabled | The rep describes workarounds. The CRM field is empty across the whole team, not for one rep. Usage drops wherever a step needs a second login or data nobody has. | Watch them do it on a live record. Count the clicks, the tabs and the waits. |
| Capable | The rep wants it and has the tools, but live attempts miss the standard. They ask for the script every time. | Set a short role-play. If they cannot do it there, it is Capable. |

Split first on whether the rep attempted the behavior live:
- **Not attempted.** No opportunity or no time is Enabled. No intent is Willing.
- **Attempted and missed the standard.** Capable. Coach it, and use the top rung of the drill ladder (a replay of the rep's own live account) to close the gap between role-play and live calls.

### Fix per leg
**Willing broken**
- Give the why in terms the rep cares about: their pipeline, their commission, the hours they get back.
- Show a visible win from a peer, with the before and after numbers.
- Start with a small willing group. On the standard adoption curve the innovators and early adopters make up about 16 percent of a population; in a sales team that is often a handful of reps. Enable them and coach them first. The rest follow when they see the win.
- Push reps and you get a few weeks of compliance. They want the change once they have the why and have seen the win.

**Enabled broken (RevOps owns this)**
- The CRM field, view or automation the behavior depends on.
- Access and licenses, granted before launch day.
- The data the rep needs at the moment of use, for example the usage trend on the renewal record.
- Protected practice time on the calendar, agreed with the manager.
- Path of Least Resistance Design (framework 8) is this leg in practice.

**Capable broken**
- Run the coaching protocol below.

### Connection to ADKAR and Traffic Light
- **ADKAR:** Desire is roughly Willing. Knowledge plus Ability is Capable. Enabled is the leg ADKAR leaves implicit. Awareness feeds Willing; Reinforcement keeps all three legs standing after launch.
- **Traffic Light:** in this skill it classifies the change by impact and sets the communication load. Willing, Capable, Enabled explains why a person or team has or has not moved. Use Traffic Light to size the rollout and the three legs to find out why adoption stalls. For a Red change, check all three legs for every affected group before launch.

## The coaching protocol (builds Capable)

The coaching protocol builds on Kyle Norton's Coaching Mastery principles (Owner.com; The Revenue Leadership Podcast newsletter, October 2024), reframed here inside Willing, Capable, Enabled: his model centers on Capable, treats Enabled in one line (make time to practice) and takes Willing as given.

Precondition: the rep trusts that you want them to win. Without it, questions read as a trap and role-play reads as an exam. A rep who trusts you is open to coaching and can still lack Willing.

The six steps: check the legs, choose the focus skill, write the standard, drill, prove it live, revisit. The numbers below are sensible defaults; adjust them to deal volume and team size.

### Step 1. Check the legs
Run the diagnosis above for this rep. Coach only when Willing and Enabled hold.

### Step 2. Choose the focus skill
Pick one skill from data, never from the last call the manager happened to hear. Read these per rep and for the team:
- **Stage-to-stage conversion.** A rep who books plenty of first meetings but turns few into qualified opportunities points at discovery.
- **Call review flags.** The pattern that recurs across several reviewed calls, not the single bad moment.
- **Methodology scores per call.** For example SPICED element scores. If Impact and Critical Event score low across a rep's last ten discovery calls, their deals carry no quantified cost and no date.
- **Talk ratio.** A rep who does most of the talking on discovery calls is pitching and learning little about the buyer.

Find the stages where this rep's conversion sits well below the team, and start with the earliest of them. A gap at an early stage carries into every later one. If discovery ends without a number on the cost of the problem, the proposal has nothing to anchor the price to. Coach negotiation at that point and you treat a symptom.

One exception: a habit that shows at every stage. Monologuing is the common one, visible as a high talk ratio on discovery, demo and negotiation calls alike. When the data shows that, coach it first.

Coach that one skill only. Anything else you notice goes on a parking list until the skill passes step 5.

### Step 3. Write the standard
Describe a good attempt in observable terms before the first drill. Examples:
- **Quantifying pain:** the buyer states the cost of the problem as a number or a range, in their own words, and the rep plays it back.
- **Renewal with falling usage:** where usage has dropped by a third, the customer names the reason before any discount comes up.

### Step 4. Drill (weekly session, 45 to 60 minutes)
Before the session the manager reviews the rep's recent calls (15 to 30 minutes) and picks one clip on the focus skill.
1. **Recheck (5 minutes).** Did the rep attempt the focus skill live since last time? If not, find out why (opportunity, time or intent) and route it to the right leg before drilling.
2. **Clip (10 minutes).** Play the recording. Coach off what happened on the call, not off recollection.
3. **Questions (10 minutes).** See "Questioning" below.
4. **Role-play (20 to 30 minutes).** See "Drill recipe" below.
5. **Close (5 minutes).** The rep names the deals where they expect to use the skill this week. You book the next session.

#### Questioning
The rep does the Analyze and Evaluate work on the recording (Bloom's levels from the Part 2 summary); you do not. Start open. Make the question more pointed only when the rep cannot find the gap. Wait after each question: the rep's search for the gap is where the learning happens. Give the answer last, and have the rep restate the next step in their own words.

Worked example, multi-threading: a late-stage deal where the rep only speaks to their champion.
- Open: "Talk me through how this decision gets made on their side."
- More pointed: "Who else signs off, and when did you last speak to each of them?"
- More pointed again: "Say your contact goes on leave next week. What happens to this deal?"
- Hint: "Whose budget line does this come out of?"
- Close: the rep says who they will meet this week, and the reason they will give for the meeting.

Worked example, quantifying pain in discovery.
- Open: "What did you learn about what this problem costs them?"
- More pointed: "Did the number come from them or from you?"
- Hint: "Which metric does your contact report to their boss each month?"

Avoid questions with the answer built in ("Shouldn't you have asked about budget?"). They tell while pretending to ask, and the rep learns to wait for the cue.

#### Drill recipe
The structure follows Ericsson's deliberate practice.
- 10 to 12 attempts of 60 to 90 seconds each, every attempt aimed at the written standard.
- One line of feedback after each attempt, on the focus skill only. Other observations go on the parking list.
- Move up one rung once the rep meets the standard twice in a row. Ladder for quantifying pain: (1) a cooperative buyer who knows the number; (2) a buyer who says they have never measured it; (3) a buyer who questions why it matters; (4) a replay of the rep's own live account, with the manager playing the real buyer.
- Return to the same skill each week until it passes step 5.

### Step 5. Prove it live (graduation rule)
A skill is finished on evidence the manager collects. The rep does not choose the evidence, and the coach's impression does not count.
1. After the first drill session, the manager pulls every recorded live attempt from the call recorder over a fixed window (default ten working days).
2. The skill graduates when four of the five most recent attempts meet the standard.
3. Fewer than five attempts in the window means the rep lacks opportunity: an Enabled signal, not a fail. Fix the opportunity (pipeline, calendar) or extend the window, and keep drilling on rung 4 meanwhile. If the skill is rare by nature (multi-threading on late-stage deals), extend the window to cover the deals in flight.
4. Below four of five: another week of drills on the same skill, then a fresh window.

### Step 6. Revisit
Book the revisit at the next fixed point in the cadence at least two weeks after graduation, for example the rep's next pipeline review. Pull the two most recent live attempts. Both at standard: take the next focus skill off the parking list. Either below: one short drill cycle, then revisit again at the following cadence point.

### Manager capacity
The coaching cadence needs its own Enabled check.
- One deep session per rep per week is 45 to 60 minutes, plus 15 to 30 minutes of call review to pick the clip and pull attempts.
- Eight reps is eight to twelve hours: one to one and a half working days a week.
- If the manager does not have that time, the coaching cadence itself is not Enabled. Fix that first: clear recurring meetings, move reporting and admin work to RevOps, or coach the small willing group deeply first. Do not launch a coaching program the calendar cannot hold.
