# Multi-entity and post-merger harmonization

## Never one plan for everyone on day one

The practice literature is unanimous even where it does not say so bluntly. Two arguments carry the most weight in a room.

**Build transition mechanisms into the design (ZS Associates).** Partial payouts on both old and new territories during handover, so customer handoffs stay clean. Payout curves more forgiving on non-legacy products than on legacy ones. Quota ramp for reps selling a product they have never sold.

**Frame it as optimisation, not harmonization (Pearl Meyer).** Harmonization merges two programs and averages them. Optimisation selects the elements that fit the combined strategy and discards the rest. The warning that lands hardest in a PE deal: the acquired company's performance was already priced into the purchase price, so a new incentive structure that ignores that means **paying twice for the same performance**.

## What can and cannot wait

| Cannot wait | Can wait |
|---|---|
| Crediting rules | Pay levels |
| Account ownership | Base salary structures |
| The dispute path | Accelerator curves |
| The plan document itself | SPIFs |
| | Plans for non-quota-carrying roles |

Ambiguity on crediting during an integration is the thing that causes immediate, unrecoverable damage. Everything in the right column can be grandfathered without anyone being harmed.

## Timeline honesty

**There is no published benchmark for how long sales comp harmonization takes.** Anyone quoting one is quoting an opinion. Say this rather than inventing a number. The real anchors:

| Anchor | Source |
|---|---|
| Comp and benefits typically held at existing levels for one year, sometimes two | Compensation Advisory Partners |
| Full comp integration "frequently requires several years" | Compensation Advisory Partners |
| Revenue synergies take about five years to capture, versus two for cost synergies | McKinsey |
| Average 23% gap between revenue synergy targets and actual attainment | McKinsey |
| Communicate new roles more than a month before year end; start comp planning before close | ZS Associates |

Vintages: the CAP and ZS items are practitioner guidance from undated publications; the McKinsey figures come from its M&A research program (2010s vintage). Treat all rows as directional, not measured.

**A defensible shape:** common architecture with entity-calibrated economics in year one, one plan family in year two. Say this early and in writing, because the alternative is inheriting someone else's promise of a unified plan by January.

## Failure modes, quantified

A sourcing caveat first: Alexander Group's own figures vary across its assets (67% lack a playbook in one, 36% have a framework in another). These are not the same survey question and neither discloses sample size. Use every row below as directional.

| Failure | Evidence |
|---|---|
| No M&A comp playbook | 67% of organizations lack one; only 36% have a written integration framework; 54% struggle to align comp practices post-M&A (Alexander Group) |
| Commercial integration treated as an afterthought | Average 7 percentage point drop in sales growth over three years post-close; more than a third fail revenue goals (McKinsey) |
| Communication vacuum | 77% of successful commercial integrators committed to clear communications versus 46% of less successful peers (McKinsey). When official communication is withheld, high performers start self-preservation behavior |
| No governance body | Over 70% of companies that met or exceeded revenue synergy goals established a commercial integration management office (McKinsey) |
| Paying twice | The acquired company's performance was already in the purchase price (Pearl Meyer) |


## CCOS as the reconciliation currency

**Compensation cost of sales** = total sales base plus variable, divided by revenue or bookings.

Cross-industry B2B benchmark: **7.9%** (Alexander Group). Cash compensation is roughly 40% of total sales costs.

**The method.** Compute CCOS for each legacy entity at actual attainment. You will get three different numbers. Design a target CCOS for the combined group. Back-solve the plan parameters to reach it.

**The seven CCOS drivers** (Alexander Group), which are also the complete list of things you are about to argue about:

1. Base pay levels
2. Target total compensation levels
3. Quota setting and allocation
4. Quota attainment levels
5. Pay curve shape
6. **Sales crediting rules**
7. Salesforce sizing

**Why this matters more than the arithmetic.** Three entities with three quota-to-OTE ratios, three curves and three crediting regimes will produce three different CCOS figures. Presenting it this way converts a fight about fairness, which the comp owner loses, into arithmetic about cost, which the comp owner wins. It is also the framing that works when the comp function reports into finance.

## Level up, level down, grandfather

**Level down is often legally unavailable in Europe.** In France and Germany, reducing variable pay is a negotiation, not a design choice. See `eu-legal-constraints.md`.

**Grandfathering is the standard bridge.** Red-circling is the mechanism: freeze an individual above the new band rather than cutting, and let band movement and non-promotion close the gap over time. CAP's observation that comp is typically held at existing levels for one to two years is institutionalised grandfathering.

**There is no public benchmark for what levelling up costs.** Mercer, WTW, Korn Ferry, SHRM and the compensation consultancies have not published it. This is a genuine gap, not a research failure. Model it three ways:

| Scenario | Definition | Who prefers it |
|---|---|---|
| Ceiling | Full level-up to the highest legacy OTE per level | Nobody, but it bounds the conversation |
| Defensible | Level-up to market median per level per country | The comp owner, HR, and eventually the works councils |
| Cash-preserving | Hold-harmless grandfathering with convergence over 24 months | The PE sponsor |

Produce this number before anyone else does. Whoever produces the first credible model owns the conversation.

## Retention during transition

| Measure | Figure | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Companies seeing higher turnover after M&A | 43% | Alexander Group |
| Companies naming retention as top M&A concern | 38% | Alexander Group |
| Firms hitting revenue goals that prioritized commercial retention plans | 82% | McKinsey |
| Share of retention effort concentrated on the critical 20% of staff | ~80% | McKinsey |
| Companies tracking or reserving fixed retention payments | 72% | WTW 2024 |
| Typical award, C-suite | 75 to 100% of base | WTW 2024 |
| Typical award, other senior leaders | 50% of base | WTW 2024 |
| Typical award, salaried employees | 30% of base | WTW 2024 |
| Salaried retention agreements that are purely time-based | 73% | WTW 2024 |
| Companies preferring cash-only awards | over 80% | WTW 2024 |

The WTW rows are from its 2024 M&A retention study; two years old by late 2026, so re-verify current award levels before quoting them into a proposal. The Alexander Group and McKinsey rows are undated survey assets; directional.

**ZS ranks performance-based retention fifth of five priorities**, effective only when the first four (plan design, communication, role clarity, territory) are handled well.

**Say this plainly when retention bonuses come up: a retention bonus does not fix a broken plan. It buys a quarter.**

## Sequencing a harmonization program

1. **Establish the constraints.** Legacy plan documents, employment contract variable-pay clauses, works council status per entity, accounting framework and capitalization policies.
2. **Measure before designing.** CCOS per entity at actual attainment. Attainment distribution per entity over four to eight quarters. Crediting rules actually in force. Dispute volume and resolution basis.
3. **Decide architecture, not mechanics.** Coverage model, job architecture, target CCOS, and the three level-up scenarios with numbers. Architecture is what triggers works council consultation, so start those workstreams here, in parallel, not after.
4. **Write the rulebook, then the mechanics.** Crediting first because it does damage while ambiguous. Then plan mechanics per role, calculation specification, governance charter.
