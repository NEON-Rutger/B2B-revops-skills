# Governance and the designer's deliverables

The named warning sign of a failing compensation function is **frequent crediting and payment disputes with no defined resolution pathway** (Alexander Group). Governance is what prevents that, and it is the part almost everyone skips because it produces no visible output until it is missing.

Supporting evidence: over 70% of companies that met or exceeded revenue synergy goals after a merger established a formal commercial integration management office (McKinsey). One executive quoted in that research described a prior integration that "lost momentum two months after close" for lack of scorekeeping.

## The three governance mechanisms

**1. A comp committee.** Comp owner, finance, HR, and the commercial leader. Monthly during a period of change, quarterly in steady state.

Its remit: approve every plan change, approve every exception, and own the annual design calendar. Exceptions are **logged**, not remembered. An exception register that nobody maintains is how a crediting model drifts from 100% to 130% over three years without anyone deciding it should.

**2. A dispute process with an SLA.**

```
Rep raises dispute through one defined channel
  -> logged with deal, claim and date
  -> resolved within a fixed SLA (typically 10 working days)
  -> resolution and reasoning recorded
  -> if novel, rulebook amended and amendment published
```

The last step is the one organizations skip, and it is the one that stops the same dispute recurring. Roughly 22% of reps hit a commission dispute in a given year and around 9% leave over commission errors (aggregated vendor survey data, directional). **Report dispute volume as a retention metric, not an admin metric.**

**3. Change control.** No verbal plan changes, ever. Every change is a versioned document with an effective date and a named approver. This is what protects the designer when someone claims a corridor promise six months later, and it is what an auditor asks for.

## The five documents a designer owes

When the designer and the administrator are different people, the deliverable is documents, not a spreadsheet.

| Document | Contents | Why it exists |
|---|---|---|
| **Design rationale** | Why this structure, what behavior it targets, cost at three attainment scenarios. Documents WHY the pay mix was chosen; the mix benchmarks themselves live in gtm-compensation | The document that survives the designer leaving |
| **Plan document, per role** | Measures, weights, curves, thresholds, payment timing, crediting, clawback, dispute process | The thing a rep signs. In France and Germany it carries legal weight. Write it as if a court will read it |
| **Crediting rulebook** | Every scenario enumerated, including the uncomfortable ones | Separate from the plan document so it can be amended without reopening contracts |
| **Calculation specification** | Field-level logic: partial quarters, mid-period joiners and leavers, amendments, currency, product mix, splits | What the administrator implements against |
| **Governance charter** | Who approves, who arbitrates, escalation path, annual calendar | Removes the ambiguity that turns every exception into a negotiation |

## The designer-administrator handover

The classic failure: the designer designs in a spreadsheet, the administrator implements an interpretation of it, and neither discovers the divergence until a rep is underpaid. By then it is a trust problem, not a calculation problem.

**Always insist on a parallel run.** One full period where the new logic is calculated alongside the old and every difference is explained line by line before anyone is paid on it. If the difference cannot be explained, the specification is incomplete.

**Write down the boundary.** What the designer hands over, what the administrator owns, and who decides when they disagree. Every undocumented assumption becomes the administrator's problem and eventually the designer's credibility problem.

## The annual calendar

A comp function with no calendar runs on emergencies. Publish these dates a year ahead:

| When | What |
|---|---|
| Q3 prior year | Design review opens. Prior-year attainment distribution analyzed |
| Q4 prior year | Architecture and mechanics agreed by comp committee. Works council consultation where required |
| Late Q4 | Plans communicated to managers, then to reps. ZS recommends more than a month before year end |
| Start of financial year | Objectives set and documented. In France this is a legal requirement, not a best practice |
| Within 30 days of start | Plan documents signed |
| Quarterly | Payout run, dispute review, exception register review |
| Mid-year | Health check only. No mechanic changes unless there is existential misalignment |

## Comp plan health check

Run any plan, inherited or designed, against these five. Report the answers rather than a verdict.

**Alignment**
- Does the plan reward behavior the strategy actually needs?
- Are comp metrics connected to revenue outcomes rather than activity?
- Do sales, marketing and CS incentives pull in the same direction?

**Simplicity**
- Can a rep calculate expected earnings in two minutes?
- Are there three or fewer variable components?
- Is the payout schedule clear and predictable?

**Fairness**
- Is there meaningful variance in payouts between top and bottom performers?
- Are quotas set on market opportunity rather than arbitrary uplift?
- Do territories offer roughly equivalent earning potential?

**Competitiveness**
- Is OTE within market range for role, level and geography?
- Is the pay mix appropriate for the level of control the role has?
- Are ramp and draw policies competitive for hiring?

**Sustainability**
- Can the company afford to pay at 120% attainment across the board?
- Is the quota-to-OTE ratio sustainable given unit economics?
- Does CCOS hold under up, flat and down revenue scenarios?

## Signals a comp function is failing

- Disputes are resolved by whoever escalates loudest.
- Exceptions exist but no register does.
- The plan document and the calculation logic have diverged and nobody knows by how much.
- Reps maintain their own shadow commission tracking. Vendor survey data puts this at around 62% where spreadsheets are used.
- Plan changes are communicated verbally.
- Nobody can state total credit as a percentage of actual revenue.
- The annual design cycle starts after the financial year has begun.
