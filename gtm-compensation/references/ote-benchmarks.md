# OTE & Compensation Benchmarks
## B2B SaaS ($15M-$150M ARR) - European Market

Last verified: 2026-09. Figures older than 2025 are kept only where no newer edition exists and are marked as such.

**Data vintage and sourcing:** This file mixes two evidence types, and every table says which one it is.

- **Sourced:** figures traced to a named publisher, shown as (Source, Year) with a URL. At the 2026-09 pass the publisher pages themselves (bridgegroupinc.com, repvue.com, ravio.com) were blocked by the research proxy, so sourced figures were taken from search-result excerpts of those pages, not from a full read of each report. Re-check the page before quoting a figure externally.
- **Practice-based:** operating estimates for $15M-$150M ARR European B2B SaaS companies that could not be traced to any published survey edition. Treat these as starting points for plan design, not as market data.

Source notes, read before using any sourced figure:
- **RepVue** medians are self-reported by sales professionals, skew toward larger and often US-headquartered software vendors, and are not segmented by ARR band. Each RepVue country page shows its own "data updated" date, quoted per figure below. Vendor/community data.
- **Ravio** figures come from Ravio's HRIS-connected European tech compensation dataset (vendor data). The figures quoted here are base salary benchmarks for a mid-level individual contributor (level P3, "Sales Executive") and a senior manager (level M3, "Sales Manager"), published in GBP with Ravio's own EUR conversion. They are base salary only, not OTE.
- **The Bridge Group** reports are US-weighted B2B surveys in USD. Use them for structure (pay mix, quota-to-OTE, ramp, attainment), not for European pay levels.
- RepVue and Ravio disagree on level: Ravio's P3 Sales Executive base in NL is €56,700, while RepVue's NL Account Executive median base is €79,250. The populations differ (Ravio: European tech companies of all sizes, one level; RepVue: self-reporting reps, all levels, larger vendors). Choose the source that matches your hiring population and say which one you used.

Amounts in the practice-based tables are in USD as in previous editions. Sourced figures are shown in the currency the source published. No USD to EUR conversion has been made in this file.

## 3. Quota-to-OTE Ratios by Role

**Definition:** Annual quota ÷ Annual OTE (the factor by which quota must exceed total earnings to ensure 100% attainment = 100% OTE earned)

Evidence type: practice-based for the table; the AE ratio has a sourced reference point below.

**Note on terminology:** Quota-to-OTE ratios vary by literature and vendor. The ratios below reflect the multiplier needed to hit OTE at full quota attainment. A 5:1 ratio means $500K quota generates $100K OTE (all variable + base combined).

The example column applies each ratio to that role's OTE Mid from the $15M-$50M ARR table in Section 1 (USD). The previous edition labeled this column "$65K OTE AE" but the values did not follow from $65K or from any single OTE; they were recomputed at the 2026-09 pass.

| Role | Typical Range | Example quota (role OTE Mid, $15M-$50M ARR) | Interpretation |
|------|---------------|---------------------------------------------|-----------------|
| SDR/BDR | 20:1 to 30:1 | $35K OTE: $700K-$1.05M pipeline value generated | Harder quota (lower personal deal value) |
| AE (SMB) | 5.5:1 to 6.5:1 | $53K OTE: $291.5K-$344.5K annual revenue | Moderate quota difficulty |
| AE (Mid-Market) | 5:1 to 6:1 | $78K OTE: $390K-$468K annual revenue | Moderate quota difficulty |
| AE (Enterprise) | 4.5:1 to 5.5:1 | $125K OTE: $562.5K-$687.5K annual revenue | Achievable quota (larger deals) |
| CSM | 7:1 to 10:1 | $50K OTE: $350K-$500K expansion revenue | Expansion-focused, lower risk |
| Sales Manager | 4.5:1 to 5.5:1 | $85K OTE: $382.5K-$467.5K (see flag) | Team leverage; lower personal close |

**Flag (unresolved):** a 4.5:1 to 5.5:1 ratio on a Sales Manager's OTE produces a quota smaller than a single Mid-Market AE's quota, which cannot be a team quota. Either the ratio is meant for a manager's personal carry, or team quota should be the sum of the reps' quotas. Confirm the intent before using this row.

**Sourced reference points:**
- AE quota-to-OTE: 4.6x median in 2026, up from 4.2x in 2024; median quota $960K on median OTE $200K (The Bridge Group, AE Models, Motions & Metrics 2026, USD, US-weighted) https://www.bridgegroupinc.com/research/2026-ae-models-motions-metrics. This sits at the low end of the table's AE ranges.
- Quota attainment, AEs: 48% of reps at quota in 2026, down from 51% in 2024 (The Bridge Group, 2026) https://www.bridgegroupinc.com/research/2026-ae-models-motions-metrics
- Quota attainment, SDRs: 60% at quota, the lowest in the study's history (The Bridge Group, 2025) https://www.bridgegroupinc.com/research/2025-sdr-models-metrics-report-the-bridge-group. RepVue estimates 63.1% of NL SDRs hit quota in the last 12 months (RepVue, 2026) https://www.repvue.com/salaries/sales-development-representative/NL and 57.9% of UK SDRs (RepVue, 2026) https://www.repvue.com/salaries/sales-development-representative/GB
- Quota attainment, cloud software sellers: 43.83% in Q4 2025 (RepVue Cloud Sales Index, Q4 2025) https://www.repvue.com/cloud-index/2025/Q4 and 43.6% across 252 companies in Q2 2026 (RepVue Cloud Sales Index Q2 2026, as announced by RepVue on X) https://x.com/thaAdamLittle/status/2080741031775969778. Note that RepVue's measure and Bridge Group's "% of reps at quota" are defined differently.

**Key Insight:** Higher OTE roles have lower quota-to-OTE ratios because:
1. Larger average deal sizes = fewer deals needed
2. Higher base ensures income stability despite longer sales cycles
3. Extended ramp periods built in

**Caveat:** Industry literature quotes this ratio multiple ways. The most practical rule: if your OTE is $100K and quota is $500K, your quota-to-OTE is 5:1. Work backwards from your target OTE to set quota.

## 4. Commission Rate Ranges by Segment

Evidence type: practice-based for the table; sourced reference point and a consistency flag below.

**First-Year Commission Rates (% of ACV)**

| Segment | SMB | Mid-Market | Enterprise |
|---------|-----|-----------|-----------|
| **SaaS Annual ACV** | $5K-$20K | $25K-$75K | $100K+ |
| **Commission Rate** | 5.5-6.5% | 4.5-5.5% | 3.5-4.5% |
| **Deal Volume Focus** | High | Medium | Low (high value) |
| **Example Deal** | $15K = $825-$975 | $50K = $2,250-$2,750 | $200K = $7,000-$9,000 |

**Sourced reference point:** median AE commission rate 11.5% of bookings at full attainment, typical range 11-14% (The Bridge Group, 2024 edition, USD, US-weighted) https://blog.bridgegroupinc.com/2024-ae-metrics-compensation-benchmark

**Flag (unresolved): the table conflicts with Sections 1-3 and with the source.** At 100% attainment the effective commission rate equals variable pay divided by quota, which is variable % divided by the quota-to-OTE ratio. Using this file's own numbers: SMB 40% ÷ 5.5-6.5 = 6.2-7.3%; Mid-Market 45% ÷ 5-6 = 7.5-9.0%; Enterprise 50% ÷ 4.5-5.5 = 9.1-11.1%. The table above shows rates falling from SMB to Enterprise, while the file's own mix and ratios imply rates rising from SMB to Enterprise, and the Bridge Group median (11.5%) is above all of them. A rate table that does not tie to variable ÷ quota will under- or over-pay OTE. Derive the rate from your own OTE, mix and quota rather than using this table directly.

**Multi-Year Deal Commission Uplift:**
- 2-year deal: +0.25% commission
- 3-year deal: +0.50% commission
- 4-year deal: +0.75% commission
- Example: $200K deal @ 4% base + 0.50% (3-yr) = 4.5% = $9,000

**Expansion/Upsell Rates:**
- SMB: 3% commission (lower deal friction)
- Mid-Market: 4% commission
- Enterprise: 5% commission (higher complexity, new relationships)

## 5. Accelerator & Decelerator Multiplier Ranges

Evidence type: practice-based.

**Standard Structure (applies when quota attainment changes):**

| Attainment Level | Accelerator | Decelerator | Multiplier |
|------------------|-------------|-------------|-----------|
| <70% | N/A | 0.50x | 50% of earned commission |
| 70-80% | N/A | 0.70x | 70% of earned commission |
| 80-100% | N/A | 1.00x | 100% of earned commission |
| 100-110% | 1.10x | N/A | 110% of earned commission |
| 110-120% | 1.25x | N/A | 125% of earned commission |
| 120%+ | 1.50x | N/A | 150% of earned commission |

**Design Notes:**
- Decelerators protect company economics at low attainment
- Accelerators reward top performers (incentivizes stretch)
- Common variant (practice-based; prevalence not measured): 1.25x at 120%, 1.50x at 130%+
- Enterprise roles may have capped accelerators (max 1.50x) due to deal size risk
- With sourced attainment of roughly 44% to 63% of reps at quota (Section 3), a decelerator band below 70% will apply to a large share of reps; model the payout curve against your own attainment distribution before adopting it.

**Monthly vs. Annual Attainment:**
- Q1-Q3: Monthly accrual possible (smooths cash flow)
- Q4: Typically annual true-up (ensure fairness, catch edge cases)

## 6. Ramp Schedule Benchmarks

Evidence type: practice-based for the table (the previous "Industry Standard" label has been removed); sourced reference points below it.

**Time to Full Quota**

| Role | Months to Full Quota | Key Milestones | Draw/Guarantee |
|------|----------------------|----------------|-----------------|
| **SDR/BDR** | 2-3 months | 4-6 wks: training + mentoring, 8-10 wks: full utilization | $2K-$3K/mo |
| **AE (SMB)** | 9-12 months | 3 mo: 40% quota, 6 mo: 60% quota, 10 mo: 100% | $1.5K-$2.5K/mo |
| **AE (Mid-Market)** | 12-15 months | 4 mo: 35% quota, 8 mo: 60% quota, 13 mo: 100% | $2K-$3K/mo |
| **AE (Enterprise)** | 15-24 months | 6 mo: 25% quota, 12 mo: 50% quota, 19 mo: 100% | $3K-$4K/mo |
| **CSM** | 6-9 months | 3 mo: 5-6 accounts, 6 mo: 10-12 accounts, 9 mo: full book | $500-$1K/mo (if variable) |
| **Sales Manager** | 3-6 months | 3 mo: team stabilization, 6 mo: team quota setting | $1.5K-$2.5K/mo |

**Sourced reference points:**
- SDR average ramp 3.0 months in 2025, the lowest since 2010; average SDR tenure 1.9 years (The Bridge Group, 2025) https://www.bridgegroupinc.com/research/2025-sdr-models-metrics-report-the-bridge-group. Consistent with the SDR row.
- AE ramp to full productivity 6.2 months in 2026, the highest in the study's history (The Bridge Group, 2026) https://www.bridgegroupinc.com/research/2026-ae-models-motions-metrics. **Flag:** the AE rows above (9-24 months) are well above this all-segment median. Enterprise ramps longer than the median, but a 9-12 month SMB ramp is hard to justify against it. Not changed, because no segment-level source was found.

**Draw Structure (most common):**
- Draw = monthly guarantee paid upfront
- Recovered from future commissions earned
- Forfeited at termination (varies by jurisdiction; check local law)
- Example: $2K/mo draw × 12 months = $24K to be recovered
- If the AE earns $40K commission in the year, $24K offsets the draw already paid and the AE receives the remaining $16K; total commission cost to the company is $40K

**Experienced Hire Ramp (roughly 20-50% faster, depending on role):**
- SDR: 4-6 weeks
- AE (SMB): 6-8 months
- AE (Mid-Market): 9-12 months
- AE (Enterprise): 12-18 months

**Data sourcing note:** The multipliers above are practice-based estimates for tech talent markets across Western Europe (2025-2026). They reflect cost of living, local talent density, and historical hiring competition. Validate them against current regional survey data (for example Ravio for European tech, Radford/Aon or Mercer subscriptions, or national statistics office wage data) or custom market data if your company has sufficient headcount in multiple regions. Regional gaps narrow and shift annually.

**Rationale:**
- Amsterdam: Highest cost of living in region, most competitive talent market for English-speaking GTM talent
- DACH: Strong economy, competing with Germany's strong tech sector demand
- UK: Post-Brexit adjustments, London premium already factored into base comparisons
- Nordics: High living costs, strong tech hubs (Stockholm, Oslo, Copenhagen), competitive for senior talent
- Rest of NL: Regional markets (Arnhem, Eindhoven, Maastricht), lower cost of living, less acute competition for junior roles

## 10. Compensation Planning Checklist

**Annual Compensation Review (Q4):**
- [ ] Review market benchmarks (Ravio for European tech, RepVue country pages for sales roles, The Bridge Group for pay mix, ratios and ramp, Radford/Aon, Mercer, Pavilion, national statistics office wage data; Payscale, Glassdoor or Levels.fyi only as labeled secondary sources)
- [ ] Assess internal equity (salary compression risks?)
- [ ] Evaluate company financial performance (budget available?)
- [ ] Conduct 1:1 reviews with individual ICs and managers
- [ ] Update OTE ranges based on market + performance
- [ ] Communicate changes by Dec 15 for new fiscal year
- [ ] Document plan changes in writing

**Quota Setting Best Practices:**
- [ ] Bottom-up territory potential calculation
- [ ] Account for market expansion vs. contraction
- [ ] Avoid excessive year-on-year swings (cap at ±15%)
- [ ] Allow 60-90 days for feedback before final lock
- [ ] Communicate quota by fiscal year start (or earlier)

**Key Metrics to Track:**
- [ ] Compensation as % of revenue (practice-based rule of thumb: 10-15% fully loaded)
- [ ] Commission cost ratio (check for runaway variable costs)
- [ ] OTE vs. actual compensation paid (is comp plan realistic?)
- [ ] Ramp success rates (are new hires hitting milestones?)
- [ ] Retention rates post-comp review (satisfaction indicator)
