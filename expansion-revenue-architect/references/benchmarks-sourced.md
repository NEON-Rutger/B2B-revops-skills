## Master Benchmark Table

Calibrated for $15-150M B2B SaaS. Always adjust for your stage, ACV, and motion type. The Good / Great / Best-in-class tiers are practice-based targets; the Source column gives the surveyed anchor each tier is set against.

| Metric | Good | Great | Best-in-class | Source |
|--------|------|-------|---------------|--------|
| GRR | >85% | >90% | >95% | Survey anchor: median 84%, top quartile 91% (Benchmarkit 2026, CY2025 data); median 91%, and 95% median for ACV >$250K (SaaS Capital 2025) |
| NRR | >100% | >110% | >120% | Survey anchor: median 102%, top quartile 110% (Benchmarkit 2026); median 101% (SaaS Capital 2025); top quartile 113-124% by ARR band (ICONIQ 2026) |
| Expansion as % of new ARR | 15-20% | 20-30% | >40% | Survey anchor: 34% at $5-20M ARR, 40% at $20-50M, majority above $50M (High Alpha); ~60% above $50M (High Alpha 2025) |
| Expansion ARR acquisition cost | $0.40/$ | $0.27/$ | $0.20/$ | Tiers practice-based. Survey anchor: median $0.63 per $1 of upsell/expansion ARR vs $1.67 for new-customer ARR (KeyBanc 2022; pre-2025, no newer published figure found) |
| CS-led expansion close rate (<$50K) | 30% | 40% | 50% | Practice-based (prior ChurnZero 2025 attribution not verified) |

Sources: Benchmarkit 2026 SaaS & AI-Native Performance Benchmarks, 342 companies, published June 2026 with Aleph (survey), https://www.benchmarkit.ai/2026-saas-ai-native-metrics ; SaaS Capital 2025 B2B SaaS Retention Benchmarks, 1,000+ private B2B SaaS companies (survey), https://www.saas-capital.com/blog-posts/what-is-a-good-retention-rate-for-a-private-saas-company/ ; ICONIQ State of Go-to-Market 2026, 150+ GTM leaders (survey), https://www.iconiq.com/growth/reports/state-of-go-to-market-2026 ; High Alpha 2025 SaaS Benchmarks Report, 800+ companies (survey), https://www.highalpha.com/saas-benchmarks ; KeyBanc Capital Markets 2022 Private SaaS Company Survey (survey), https://www.key.com/content/dam/kco/documents/businesses___institutions/2022_kbcm_saas_survey_10-20-22_vF.pdf

## The Expansion Economics Advantage

Expansion revenue is the most efficient growth lever in B2B SaaS.

### Cost Efficiency

| Metric | New Business | Expansion | Multiple | Source |
|--------|-------------|-----------|----------|--------|
| CAC (cost to acquire $1 of ARR) | $1.67 | $0.63 | **~2.7x cheaper** | KeyBanc 2022 Private SaaS Company Survey, medians (survey; pre-2025, no newer published figure found) |
| Payback period | 18-24 months | ~6 months | **3-4x faster** | Practice-based (prior Paddle 2024 attribution not verified) |
| Close rate | 5-20% | 60-70% | **3-10x higher** | Practice-based (prior Gainsight 2024 attribution not verified) |
| Sales cycle | 60-180 days | 14-90 days | **2-4x shorter** | Practice-based (prior 180ops 2024 attribution not verified) |

### Expansion Revenue Share by ARR Stage

As companies scale, expansion becomes the dominant growth source:

| ARR Stage | % from New Business | % from Expansion | Source |
|-----------|-------------------|-----------------|--------|
| $1-5M | ~77% | ~23% | High Alpha, "How Expansion Revenue Drives Sustainable SaaS Growth" (SaaS Benchmarks data; edition year not stated) |
| $5-20M | ~66% | ~34% | High Alpha (as above) |
| $20-50M | ~60% | ~40% | High Alpha (as above) |
| >$50M | ~40% | ~60% | High Alpha 2025 SaaS Benchmarks Report |

Source URLs: https://www.highalpha.com/blog/how-expansion-revenue-drives-sustainable-saas-growth and https://www.highalpha.com/saas-benchmarks . The High Alpha blog phrases the $1-50M figures as expansion's share of revenue; they are read here as share of new ARR, consistent with the 2025 report's "~60% of new ARR from existing customers" above $50M. Verify against the report before quoting externally.

Counterpoint (ICONIQ State of Go-to-Market 2026, survey): among its fastest-growing "Pacesetter" companies, roughly 70%+ of gross new ARR still comes from new logos, so a high expansion share is a scale effect, not a substitute for new-logo growth.

(Removed in the 2026-09 refresh: <$1M, $50-100M and $200M+ rows sourced to OpenView-era benchmarks from 2023 or earlier, and the Ordway Labs 2024 $20-50M row, superseded by High Alpha.)

**Cost-of-gap argument:** If your company at $30M ARR is getting only 15% of new ARR from expansion (vs. ~40% benchmark at $20-50M, High Alpha), that's a 25pp gap. At a $6M new ARR target, that gap = $1.5M in missed expansion revenue annually, at roughly 2.7x lower CAC than acquiring it via new business (KeyBanc 2022 medians).
