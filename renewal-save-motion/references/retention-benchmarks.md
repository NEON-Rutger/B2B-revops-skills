# Retention Benchmarks: Provenance and How the Skill Uses Them

Last verified: 2026-09. Figures older than 2025 are kept only where no newer edition exists and are marked as such.

Every number in the skill carries a source and a vintage; retention benchmarks drift with the funding climate, so vintage matters. Recalibrate against current-year surveys where you have access.

## Gross revenue retention (GRR)

- Median 84%, top quartile (75th percentile) 91%, across 226 reporting B2B SaaS and AI-native companies, CY2025 actuals; down from 88% median and 95% top quartile in the prior edition (Benchmarkit 2026 SaaS & AI-Native Performance Benchmarks, published June 2026 with Aleph; survey). https://www.benchmarkit.ai/2026-saas-ai-native-metrics
- By go-to-market motion, median GRR is 88% for assisted (sales-led), 80% for hybrid and 79% for self-serve (Benchmarkit 2026; survey).
- Median 91% across 1,000+ private B2B SaaS companies; 92% for bootstrapped and 90% for equity-backed companies; companies with ACV above $250K post a 95% median, below that ~91% (SaaS Capital 2025 B2B SaaS Retention Benchmarks, 14th annual survey; survey). https://www.saas-capital.com/blog-posts/what-is-a-good-retention-rate-for-a-private-saas-company/ and https://www.saas-capital.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/09/RB32WS1-2025-B2B-SaaS-Retention-Benchmarks.pdf
- SaaS Capital frames 90% GRR as table stakes for performance parity with peers (SaaS Capital, 2025).
- Gross retention fell to 86% in 2023 and is expected to approach 90% (KeyBanc Capital Markets and Sapphire Ventures SaaS Survey, 16th edition, 2025; survey). https://info.sapphireventures.com/2025-keybanc-capital-markets-sapphire-ventures-saas-survey

How to read the spread: the two main 2025-26 surveys disagree by about seven points at the median (84% vs 91%) because their samples differ (Benchmarkit includes AI-native and self-serve companies; SaaS Capital surveys private B2B SaaS only). Use 84% to 91% as the median band and ~91% as the top-quartile line, and treat 95% as enterprise-grade (the >$250K ACV median), not as a general top-quartile bar. GRR strips expansion out and answers one question: how much revenue did you keep? The skill scores the save motion against this line because expansion cannot be allowed to camouflage leakage.

## Net revenue retention (NRR)

- Median 102%, top quartile 110% (230 reporting companies, CY2025 actuals) (Benchmarkit 2026; survey). https://www.benchmarkit.ai/2026-saas-ai-native-metrics
- Median 101% across private B2B SaaS; 104% bootstrapped, 101% equity-backed (SaaS Capital 2025; survey). https://www.saas-capital.com/blog-posts/what-is-a-good-retention-rate-for-a-private-saas-company/
- ACV gradient: companies with $25K to $50K ACV post median NRR 102%, top quartile 111%, bottom quartile 97%; higher ACV bands post higher and tighter NRR (SaaS Capital 2025; survey).
- Top-quartile NRR by ARR band: 113% under $50M ARR, 124% at $50M to $100M, 115% at $100M+ (ICONIQ State of Go-to-Market 2026, 150+ GTM leaders; survey). https://www.iconiq.com/growth/reports/state-of-go-to-market-2026

The skill references NRR only for context; the save motion is a GRR instrument. Segment any target against your ACV band, not the blended headline. (Removed in the 2026-09 refresh: an enterprise 118% / mid-market 108% / SMB 97% NRR split previously labeled "2025 industry aggregations"; it traces only to aggregator blogs, not to a primary survey.)

## Retention vs acquisition economics

- Acquiring a new customer costs five to 25 times more than retaining an existing one, depending on study and industry (Harvard Business Review, Amy Gallo, "The Value of Keeping the Right Customers", 2014; pre-2025, no newer edition). https://hbr.org/2014/10/the-value-of-keeping-the-right-customers The same article cites Bain & Company (Frederick Reichheld) research that a 5% increase in retention raises profits by 25% to 95%. The skill uses the conservative end (5x+) and only to justify staffing a save motion at all, never to price a specific concession.

## Practice-based rules (no external study)

Flagged in the skill as practice-based, not benchmarked:

- Risk is visible in system behavior (usage decay, sponsor silence) well before it is spoken; discovering risk inside T-60 indicates a detection failure.
- Scope right-sizing retains more two-year revenue than first-lever discounting.

Both come from operator experience across B2B scale-up engagements. Treat them as defaults to test against your own cohort data, and replace them with your measured numbers as soon as two quarters of save-motion scorekeeping exist.
